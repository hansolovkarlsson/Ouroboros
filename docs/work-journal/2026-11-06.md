## Step 1: the driver on QEMU, and two mutations an emulator cannot fail

`kernel/src/sdhci.rs` is an SD card brought up from scratch by the SD spec
on a standard SDHCI and read by PIO, one sector at a time, through 32-bit
register accesses only. QEMU's firmware will not boot from an SD card. It
falls through to its shell. So `make test-sd` boots the ESP from
`virtio-blk-pci`, which the firmware reads and the kernel cannot, and puts
`disk.img` on `sdhci-pci`. That is the Pi's own shape, a firmware disk and a
root disk. The card goes in only when no other disk did, so no configuration
that already booted changes. There are two cards, because QEMU makes a
128 MiB card standard-capacity (byte-addressed) and a 4 GiB card
high-capacity (block-addressed), and the address rule is where a driver
goes wrong first.

The plan named three mutations, and two of them could not fail. QEMU models
no data lines, so a driver that never sends ACMD6 and leaves the card 1-bit
while the controller runs 4-bit passes. I ran it, and it did pass. There
is also no multi-block read to take the CMD12 from. They were replaced by
three that do fail: CMD7 with no RCA, byte addressing always, and block
addressing always. The bus width was written down as the Pi's to show. The
high review then added a reset of the command and data lines after a
timeout, voltage before bus power, and one `take_from_firmware` shared with
the xHCI. The Pi booted that build with its old card and stick, and the
shared path took the xHCI with the same command register values as before.

## Step 2: writes, and a refusal that would have bricked the Pi's card

CMD24 writes one sector at a time, under the same address rule. D3 says the
firmware partition is never written, so the refused range is fixed at
bring-up by `parttable::firmware_partition` and checked before the
controller is touched. The rig writes `/sdnote`, powers off, reads it back
on a second boot, then runs `partition fat32` and `format fat32`, and the
format's first write is refused.

The review found that the first version protected the partition and not the
table that locates it. `partition fat32` at sector 0 was allowed, and on a
Pi card it would have left `RPI_EFI.fd`'s sectors whole and unreachable,
which is a card that does not boot. `parttable::firmware_sectors` now
protects sector 0 through the partition's end plus a GPT's backup copy, and
a card that is not blank but lists nothing is kept whole. A write's
completion not waited for was a third mutation QEMU could not fail, since it
finishes a write at once. That went on the Pi's list too.

## Step 3: the Pi reads its card, first try

With no SD controller on PCI, the kernel takes the one EMMC2 the ACPI
tables declare (`BRCME88C` at `0xFE340000`, found by `amlscan` yesterday)
and brings the card up after the exit whether it is installed or not. On
the board (`385f6d9d`): SDHCI 3.00, a 100 MHz base clock, a 119 GiB
high-capacity card identified at 400 kHz and then run 4-bit at 25 MHz. So
the power sequence, the 20 µs waits Linux's `sdhci-iproc` uses on the
BCM2711, ACMD6 and its card status all held on hardware on the first boot.
The card was named the boot disk at LBA 32768, matching the firmware's
device path, and the stick stayed the root, as the step required.

## Step 4: the card alone, then the card ahead of the stick

`make bootstick PI=1` had refused a Pi card because the kernel could not
read one. Now it takes it, carrying the card's own `RPI_EFI.fd` whole
because its settings route the card to EMMC2, where the pinned release's
might route it to Arasan (D4). Hans staged the card with `sudo` on the Mac
and booted it with no stick (`0f6f471a`). The ext2 root mounted off the
card at its full 244,277,248 sectors, and the login, `bootid` and `/proc`
worked. On the next round trip he wrote `/card`, shut down by PSCI,
power-cycled the board, and boot 3 read the file back. Those were the
driver's first writes on hardware, through ext2, and the protected range
refused nothing it should have taken.

The card with a stick in a port needed a ranking rule. `init_sd` now brings
the card up before the USB step, and a card that is the boot disk goes in
there, ahead of the stick. The Pi boot of that build (`b6b78bee`, only the
kernel and its stamp copied onto the card) installed the card, left the
stick out, and read `/card` again. That is the arc's Done when: the Pi
boots and runs from its card alone, and a stick no longer takes the root.

## Two reviews, and the gap between them

The high review of steps 3 and 4 found that `bootstick` would take any card
holding `RPI_EFI.fd`, another OS's included. It now wants
`EFI/ORBS/INIT.CFG` too. A GPT card's backup is now protected where its
headers put it (`parttable::gpt_backup_extent`), not assumed at the end.

Then I found a gap the review had not named. A card as `make sdcard`
stages it is the boot disk too, and the new rule put its firmware
partition, every sector of it refused, ahead of the stick that holds its
root. The rig's seventh boot had tested only the layout the rule was
written for. A boot card now ranks ahead only with a root past its firmware.
The second review found that test was itself a second copy of fsd's root
rule and already disagreed with it: it took only typed roots where fsd
falls back to an untyped partition, and it took a typed root with no
filesystem on it. `parttable::root_candidates` is now the one rule. fsd's
`root_order` is built on it, the kernel ranks by it, and a candidate counts
only with an ext2, FAT32 or exFAT mark. The eighth and ninth boots of
`make test-sd` are the two layouts that tell the rules apart, and each
fails under the mutation aimed at it. Two findings were declined with
reasons recorded in the plan. One ranking function for every disk waits
for a fourth disk kind. A stick that attaches after the boot scan is step
3's rule and `mount -a`'s.

There were wrong turns of my own along the way. The first "kept whole"
check compared the protected range with the whole card, which misfired for
a card whose single partition runs to its last sector, and it became a flag
from the match that decides it. I wrote that EMMC2 is the firmware
release's default routing, when the sources disagree, and the docs now say
so. I wrote "slow by design" of PIO with no measurement behind it, and took
it out. And I edited the Makefile, which the stale-image guard hashes, in
the middle of a mutation run. It did not trip the guard that time, but it
could have voided the run.
