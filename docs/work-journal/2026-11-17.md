# 2026-11-17

## The closeout merged, and `load_auth`'s wait

The day began with yesterday's closeout merged as #476. Its standup put
one fix first: `load_auth` and `load_netconf` retried with `NET_WAIT(40)`
as a 40 ms sleep, and since #475 `NET_WAIT` returns at once when a frame
or a message is waiting. On QEMU, with `test-blank-disk`'s blank disk and
`cpu` typed during the load, the 2 s budget ran out in 1327 ms against
2435 ms with nothing typed. The fix (#477) makes the budget a deadline on
`MONOTONIC_US` and each pause a `SLEEP_UNTIL`: 2033 ms with the message
and 2037 ms without.

The first fix was a different one, and a wrong turn. The roadmap's own
entry proposed answering the mailbox while the load waited, so a queued
message could not cut the sleep short. Built, it sent the shell 72 bytes
of status words as `cpu` output: `NETOP_RUN` replies with text, and a
status word answered in its place is read as the run's output. The same
attempt showed that the supervisor's ping is never sent during a timed
sleep, which the roadmap entry had said it was. The mailbox is left as it
is, and `serve` answers the queued `cpu` after the load.

The review found the rig weaker than it looked. `test-blank-disk`'s LOAD
case checked that the wait lasted at least 1900 ms, and the `NET_WAIT`
mutation passed it: with a deadline the budget holds whatever the wait
does, and the early return only spins inside it, 2702 retries in 2003 ms,
each a read of `fsd`. The case now also bounds the retries at 60, and
the mutation fails. A second review found three records that still told
the old story (`ROADMAP.md`'s open gap, `testing-parallels.md`'s risk 1
and `testing-pi4.md`'s risk 4), each rewritten to say what #477 did. The
first boot on the Pi after the merge (#478) showed no retry and no
regression; the card mounted in time, as it always had.

## The late cable

Next on the list: a cable plugged in after the boot's 5 s link wait left
the board deaf. Decision D3 had the link asked again only at a send, a
rule made for a transmitter, and `netd` sends nothing unprompted, so a
Mac's ARP went unanswered until someone typed `ping` on the Pi. Hans
chose to have `NET_WAIT` ask over asking from the tick (which may not
write or wait) and over a `netd` announcement. The wait now asks the
link at most once a second while the MAC is off and starts the MAC when
the link is up (#479).

The control was a boot of `main` with the cable out until after `login:`
and nothing typed on the Pi (boot 37): no link line, `curl` 000, the
Mac's ARP entry incomplete. The fix answered: `link up at a wait`, `curl`
200, the ARP entry the Pi's MAC. The review then found the first version
asking about every two seconds, because the wait ends on a 20 ms tick and
was told a whole second again each time, and stopping for good at one
failed MDIO read. The rule moved into the `genet` crate as `linkask`,
host-tested: once a second, and stopped after three failed asks running.
The reviewed build (boot 38) found the link while the board sat at
`login:` with nobody logged in.

## The receiver's pause frames

The receiver is never turned off, so with nobody draining it the ring
fills, and whether the MAC then sends PAUSE frames was not known. Boots
39 to 41 answered it with `netd` left off the card and a temporary line
reading the MAC's own counters every 5 s. `main` sent about 30 pause
frames a second from the second reading on, with or without a flood from
the Mac, 10365 in six minutes. Boot 40's diagnostic was too narrow to see
the frames arrive and proved nothing either way. Hans chose ignoring
pause over turning the receiver off; every `Mac::start` now sets both
pause-ignore bits, and boot 41 sent none while the MAC received 20081
frames and the full ring discarded 19813 (#480).

The review corrected the citation. I had written that Linux sets both
bits when nothing is negotiated, in `bcmgenet_mii_setup`; it sets each
bit from its own negotiated direction, in `bcmgenet_mac_config`. That
turned a claim into an open question this driver cannot yet answer:
whether the PHY's default advertisement offers pause was never read.

## The drain loop, on the board

The last item of the day was `netd`'s drain loop, which took frames for
as long as any waited. A control on `main` (boot 42): `curl` answered,
then a 15 s UDP flood from the Mac, `scratch/udp_flood.py`. About 65,000
frames in, the supervisor called `netd` wedged, restarted it three
times, each wedged again by the flood, and gave up on it for the boot.
The second `curl` hung. QEMU showed the same through a forwarded UDP
port, which meant a rig could guard it, and `test-net-flood` was written
to fail on `main` before the fix went in.

Hans chose a bound with progress at `NET_WAIT` over a heartbeat inside
`netd`'s loop. The bound alone is not enough: under a flood `NET_WAIT`
returns at once, so `netd` is never seen blocked, and only `MSG_RECV`
had counted as progress. The first version counted every `NET_WAIT`,
and passed the rig. The review found what that rule hid: a `netd`
spinning through `NET_WAIT` and taking nothing would never be restarted.
So a pass is progress only when it took input, marked at `NET_RECV` and
read at `NET_WAIT` (`supervisor::note_pass`), and the rig gained its
other half, a restart-rig `netd` that spins on `NET_WAIT` and must be
called wedged. The controls ran both ways: the old rule passed the flood
and missed the spin, a rule that never marks failed the flood with four
wedges.
