## The morning: #221's premise was false (#221)

The day began from the 10-05 evening standup: #221, step 3 of the C-hosting
plan (`environ`), open and not yet reviewed. Its high review found nine
things, and the first was that the PR's reason for existing was wrong. It
said a picolibc program calling `getenv` did not link until `crt0` defined
`environ`. It did link: picolibc's own `libc.a` defines `environ`
(`libc_stdlib_environ.c.o`) as an empty vector, so `getenv` linked all along
and answered every name as unset. I confirmed it with `llvm-nm` before
believing it. The wrong account had started in the plan itself, in its
`llvm-nm` paragraph from #217, and was copied into five records; all five now
say what happened, and the plan's paragraph carries a dated correction rather
than a silent edit.

The same review found `crt0` defining a second, strong `environ`, which only
linked because picolibc's archive member was skipped, and would have broken a
ported program that defines its own. It is declared now, and the hand-rolled
libc defines it weakly. It also found that the wire check had never compared
a syscall number: `check-wire-constants.py` matches names exactly, and the C
header spells `SYS_GET_ENV` where the Rust says `GET_ENV`. All fifteen are
compared now, with a floor. And `test-cenv` took its expected values from
`printenv`, the program it was comparing against; they are its own now.

The seventh finding was a kernel question, not a libc one: `GET_ENV` refused
an out buffer over the blanket 512-byte cap, although the store it reads is
2048, which is why `crt0` carried a copy of the kernel's private
`MAX_USER_LEN`. Hans had that split into its own PR, stacked on #221: #222.

## #222: refuse, then cap, then one helper (#222)

#222 went through three high reviews, and the shape of the fix changed twice.
The first version kept the refusal and moved its threshold to the store's
size, so the manual's "longer buffers are rejected, not truncated" stayed
true. The first review argued that was the wrong rule for an OUTPUT buffer,
whose spare room the kernel never writes, and that a refusal reading as "no
such entry" was the real defect. I agreed, and Hans chose capping. The second
version capped at the store's size, for all four store reads, through one
helper. Its review found `TASK_NAME` reading the same argv store and still
refusing, `env_at` returning a length callers would slice past (and panic),
and the CHANGELOG entry pasted into the middle of #221's. `env_at` now takes a
buffer the size of the store and returns the entry itself, so a short buffer
cannot be spelled. The third review found five arms each repeating the bound
and the unsafe write, and a containment check over the capped length rather
than the bytes written; one `copy_out` does both now, checking only what it
copies, so no capacity is too large.

Three of the five arms could not be told from the old refusal by any rig,
since every reader passed 512 bytes or less. `/bin/RDPROBE`, a probe and not
a tool, reads all five stores through a 4 KiB buffer, and `test-cenv` runs it
after a `cd` and a `bind`; with the old refusal put back, each of its five
read checks fails on its own. Before the probe I had made `/bin/ARGS`'s buffer
oversized as a test hook, which the third review rightly called fragile; the
probe replaced it. `ulib::arg` truncates the same way `env_at` did, and Hans
kept that for its own PR: it has a caller in nearly every `/bin` tool.

## `stat(path)`, and a `/net` route taken out again (#223)

The plan said to build `stat` from `open`, `fstat` and `close`. That needs
read permission on the file and a free fd, and POSIX's `stat` needs neither;
fsd already authorizes `NP_STAT`, the path verb `ls -l` sends, by the ancestor
walk alone. So `stat` is one `NP_STAT`. The control that shows the
difference: built the plan's way, the full-fd-table check and the ordinary
user's `stat` of `/etc/shadow` fail, and nothing else does.

Its first review found `stat` refusing `/net` and the console with `ENOSYS`,
as `open` does. I routed `/net` to `netd`. Its second review found that
`netd` answers `NP_STAT` only for `/`, `ip` and `mac`; under `/tcp` the
request reached the dial files' handler and existing files got `EISDIR`,
`EIO` or `ENOENT`. That was my fix growing past its bug, and I recommended
taking `/net` back out rather than fixing `netd` inside a libc PR: `/net`
answers `ENOSYS` again, and `netd`'s gap is on the roadmap. The same round
found a trailing slash collapsed away (fixed: it must name a directory) and
`..` resolved as text, Plan 9 style, before the server sees the path, which
is a decision for the whole library and went to the roadmap instead. The
independent-values check added in round one found the ext2 image's
`/etc/shadow` owned by uid 501, gid 20: `mke2fs -d` copies the build host's
owner, the long-carried "ext2 uid 501" item. The rig reads the expected owner
off the image with `debugfs` now, not from whoever runs it.

## The clock (#224)

`gettimeofday` and `clock_gettime` read `MONOTONIC_US`, so `time()` links and
says 1970 plus uptime; there is no wall clock. picolibc's `features.h` defines
`_POSIX_MONOTONIC_CLOCK` for RTEMS alone, so `<time.h>` hid `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`;
the review moved the fix from a magic number in two files to one flag on every
picolibc compile line. The check that matters is the host's clock: two runs
with a gap the host times. A control clock 1000 times slow passed all nine of
`CCLOCK`'s own checks and both date-format checks, and only the pace failed.
The review found the pace check's slack (2 s over 9) wide enough to pass a
clock 20% wrong. My first tightening still passed a control 5% fast; the
second, a 20 s gap and 0.3 s plus 1%, fails it.

## The headers on the disk, and a FAT32 name's case (#225)

Step 6 stages picolibc's 136 headers and a generated `target.h` in `/include`
and clang's freestanding headers in `/include/clang`, 168 files, on all three
image types. Looking at them on the guest showed `stdio.h` listed as
`STDIO.H` while `syslimits.h` kept its case: macOS writes a lowercase name
that fits 8.3 as a short entry with byte 12's case flags and no long name, and
fsd ignored the flags. I fixed the read side. The first review found the write
side still uppercasing every name fsd creates, so `touch foo.txt` listed as
`FOO.TXT` beside the image's `stdio.h`, a new inconsistency the read fix had
made; fsd writes the flags now, and a mixed-case name goes to a long name.

The rig found an older gap on its first run: `ls /include` shows about 60 of
72 names. `NP_READDIR` has no offset and fills one 512-byte reply, skipping
any name that no longer fits and going on, so a cut listing has holes, not a
missing tail. I first wrote that it stopped at the first name that did not
fit; the second review corrected me, which matters to whoever designs the
paged verb. It is on the roadmap, with `mv`'s refusal of a case-only rename
without `-f` (the destination it finds is the source) and two older rename
defects the review found in fsd.

## `/bin/cpp`: the finish line (#226)

DevTools's `cpp` linked against this runtime the first time, 104 KB, no
warnings: steps 1 to 5 had supplied every call it makes. On a booted image
`cpp hello.c`, with no options, prints the program, and `cpp -o` of
`picodemo.c`, a dozen headers deep, writes 19,567 bytes that are byte for
byte what the same `cpp` writes on the Mac from the same headers. The plan's
finish line said "the same bytes as `clang -E`", which cannot hold (clang's
spacing and line markers differ); the byte comparison is with `cpp` on the
host, and `cpp` against clang, token for token, is DevTools's own
`pico-check`. Step 7 needed nothing: the 1 MiB heap held it.

It took three high reviews, and most of each round's findings were in the
round before's fixes. Round two found that the rig read QEMU's fault trace
before QEMU had flushed it; I moved four rigs' reads after `stop()`. Round
three found `stop()` sent SIGKILL, which throws the buffer away, so the move
fixed nothing; the fix is now in `drive-qemu.py` once, for every rig: a
graceful stop, and `aborts()` stopping the guest itself. Round two also had
me name a build switch `CPP`, which make predefines as the preprocessor
command, so my default never took and `CPP=required` would have reached every
tool that runs `$CPP`; it is `NEED_CPP`. And round two's high-byte input,
with the reference forced to unsigned `char`, could not catch the sign bug it
was for; the reference is built both ways now. After that round I suggested,
and Hans ran, a low review instead of a fourth high one; it found nothing.
