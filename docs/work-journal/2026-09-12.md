## 2026-09-12: fid-verbs step 5, the export half, built and then hardened by two reviews

Five days after the 09-07 closeout, and the tree was exactly where that
standup left it: `main` at `932bcc0` plus its merge, clean, and the
`roadmap-fid-verbs.md` plan already written through step 7. So the day had a
named next move and took it: **step 5, the export half.** By its close the
export serves `NP_OPEN`, `NP_FSTAT` and `NP_CLUNK` on a session, across two
merged pull requests (#128, #129), and the reasoning below is mostly about the
things that are not in the diff: what the reviews found, which of their
findings were real, and the one they got backwards.

### What step 5 is, and the shape the plan already fixed

A *fid* is 9P's open-file handle, the server-side counterpart to a POSIX fd. A
client opens a path once, reads and writes through the returned handle, and
clunks it to close. `fsd` has implemented all five fid verbs since the libc
arc; the export (`netd`'s 9P-over-TCP gateway) implemented none, so a C
program's `open()` on a *remote* mount had nothing to answer it. Step 4
(09-07) built the piece that made an export fid possible at all: a **session**,
a connection held across requests, because a fid needs a lifetime and every
remote request before that opened its own connection and closed it. Step 5
hangs the handles off that session.

Two decisions from the plan did the load-bearing work before a line was
written. **Decision 2**, `netd` owns the fids: a remote client names `netd`'s
number (`FID_BASE + slot`), never `fsd`'s, because `fsd`'s ownership check is
keyed on the calling task slot and every export request arrives as the same
slot (`NET_TASK`), so `fsd` alone could not tell two remote clients apart. The
fid table therefore lives in `TcpConn`, four slots, each recording the `fsd`
fid behind it and the remote user who opened it. **Step 2's trap**, carried in
the plan in block capitals: `NP_OPEN`'s parameters are reversed from every
other path verb (`a0` is the open flags, `a1` the path length), so it cannot
go through the generic path decode. Both were known going in, which is why the
build itself was uneventful.

### The gate, and why a mutation is the real test

The export half went in behind a new `np9p_client.py fid-gate`: open, fstat,
clunk over one session, the `fstat` record byte-compared against `NP_STAT`'s
for the same path, then the three negative controls the plan named (a fid
never opened is refused; a fid from session A is refused on session B; closing
a session leaves its numbers dead on the next). Run under `run-guest.sh`, not
`drive-qemu.py`, because the blind-instruments lesson from 09-07 is still the
freshest scar: a host client outliving the guest reads a killed process as a
refusing one. Against `main` first, to watch it fail: 8 of 10, the two
boundary pins passing by construction. Against the branch: 10 of 10, guest
alive, zero aborts.

The check that matters most is the one a per-connection table cannot pass on
its own. Closing a session must clunk its fids *on `fsd`*, or `fsd`'s own
eight-slot table leaks until it restarts. A per-connection table refuses a
stale number whether or not `fsd` was ever told, so "an old number is dead on
a fresh session" proves nothing about `fsd`. The gate adds the half that can
fail: twelve opens across three closed sessions against `fsd`'s eight slots,
which succeed only if each close really clunked. Two mutations confirmed the
checks bite: remove the `Drop` that clunks the table and the twelve-opens
check fails at the eighth; remove the per-user check and `fsd` drops the
co-tenant's fid, so the owner's next `fstat` fails too. A gate whose checks
have not each been watched to fail is a gate that proves nothing, and that was
the whole of the 09-07 finding.

### The high review, and which of ten findings were real

The `high` review returned ten findings. Six were worth fixing on the spot and
went into #128 alongside the feature: the never-opened control was probing the
same fid it had just clunked (identical to the check above it, could not fail
independently); an unserved verb logged `one-shot` where a reader greps for
`export`; `FS_ERR_NO_SUCH_VERB` now carries a second meaning ("not on this
kind of connection") that its doc and the client messages did not mention;
`Drop`'s doc read as the connection's whole teardown when a running `cpu`
child is not released there; the plan's claim that Decision 2 "makes 8 an
`fsd`-local number again" was false, since every remote fid is also an `fsd`
fid. Small, all of them, and all the same shape: a claim that went stale the
moment the code around it moved.

Three more were the real defects, and all three are the same underlying fact
seen from different angles: **an `fsd` fid can outlive what `netd` knows about
it.** A `netd` restart leaves `fsd` holding fids owned by a `NET_TASK` slot
that is alive again the instant the fresh `netd` loads, so `fsd` never reaps
them. An `fsd` restart wipes its table and reissues numbers from three, so a
session's cached number now names a different file. And `fsd`'s ownership rule,
written for a sender that is never recycled, treats a uid mismatch as a
recycled slot and drops the fid. The first and third are an `fsd`-side change
(the slot-as-identity ledger item), deferred to their own PR. The second,
`fsd`-restart aliasing, went into #129: a `SessionFid` now records
`TASK_IDENTITY(FSD_TASK)` at open, and every op and `Drop` compares it against
`fsd`'s current generation, treating a mismatch as a dead handle.

### The item the reviews disagreed about, and the race with a safe direction

Two things are worth carrying past this arc.

The first is **item 9, the refactor I chose not to do.** Folding the fid
dispatch into `build_9p_reply` is a real deduplication, but its payoff is step
6's: `NP_PREAD` would become one arm beside `NP_READ_AT`, reusing the chunk
machinery that lives only in `build_9p_reply`. Doing it now means touching the
dispatch preamble every path verb runs through, which the fid gate does not
exercise, for no behavioural gain today. Hans asked which choice fit the
standing policy, stable before secure before open-to-expansion, and the
answer was the same on all three axes: defer. It is safest (the blast radius
exceeds the test coverage here), neutral on security, and *better* for future
expansion, because step 6 brings both the consumer that justifies the fold and
the read-path gate that would cover it. The merge-with-consumer lesson from
`cluster-phase0-postmortem.md`, landed against. Recorded at step 6 in the
plan so it is not re-litigated.

The second is **a race whose obvious fix reintroduces the bug.** The `low`
review, run over #129, flagged the generation guard: `netd` reads `fsd`'s
generation *before* the open call and records that, and the review called it a
latent bug, suggesting the read move to after the open. It should not. There
is no atomic "open and report the generation", so an `fsd` restart in the
window is recorded wrong whichever side the read lands, and the two directions
are not equal. Reading before records the old generation against a fid on the
new one, so a later op sees a mismatch and refuses a *live* fid: the client
re-opens, harmless. Reading after would record the new generation against a
fid opened on the old, now-wiped `fsd`, so a later op sees a match and forwards
a stale number `fsd` may have reissued, which is the exact aliasing the guard
exists to stop. The guard must fail toward false-dead, never false-live. The
ordering stayed; a comment now records why, so the next reviewer does not make
the swap. A well-meant "obvious fix" that quietly undoes the defence is its own
small hazard, and the defence against it is the same as always: say, in the
code, why the non-obvious thing is the right one.

### Where it ends

`main` at the close serves the read-capable fid verbs over a session,
`MAX_FIDS` is a shared `ninep-abi` constant the wire checker pins and whose
budget the gate's rounds must exceed (a number that was quoted in six places
and computed nowhere, which would have let a raised table leave the gate's one
fallible check passing with the clunk deleted). What is left: the **client
half** of step 5, where the plan's one sentence hid a design decision rather
than a follow-up, `netd` must *hold* a client-side session across a C
program's `open()` to `close()`, which `tcp_get`'s one-connection-per-call
model cannot do and `mount -r` never tells `netd` about; step 6 (`NP_PREAD`,
carrying the item-9 fold); and the `fsd`-side ownership change that review
items 10 and 11 share.
