# 2026-10-25

## The disk-trace rigs onto `boot_stalled`, and five reviews of a rule for a case of boots

The day opened with the closeout of the 24th merged (#349) and the
standup's first fix: on the 24th `test-write-walk` and `test-spawn-reads`
had each reported a boot QEMU's firmware never started as a FAIL, and each
passed on a rerun. Eight rigs already sent their boots through
`drive-qemu.py`'s `boot_stalled`, so the fix looked like the pattern copied
twice. The first commit (`d83d6330`) did that through `disktrace.boot`,
which both rigs use: given the rig's name, a stalled boot raised `Stalled`
in place of its counts, and the rig caught it around the case.

That framing was wrong, and it took the first max review to say how. These
rigs do not grade a boot; they grade a pair, a base boot against a graded
one, and catching the stall around the pair threw away whatever the other
boot had found. A first boot that failed, beside a second that stalled,
exited 100 with "no check failed". And since the stall log keyed a boot by
a label recovered from its tag, a hang every run meets took five runs to be
graded, not `PERSISTENT`'s three. The rule the rework settled on is the
one the rest of the branch kept: a boot that ran and failed fails its case
whatever the other boots did, and a case is INCONCLUSIVE only when a boot
stalled and none failed. `boot` answers None for a stall instead of raising,
every boot carries `rig:name` as its label, and all four disk-trace rigs
(`test-listing-walk` and `test-dir-scan` too) moved onto it.
`disktrace.py --self-test`, a fake guest on a scratch `build/`, went into
`make test`, and its first version passed one of its own mutants; a check
was added for it (`0dfdf865`) before the push.

The second max review found the same defect a layer further out, in three of
the four rigs: problems a rig found after its boots (write-walk's inspect of
the copy, spawn-reads' chain checks, dir-scan's bounds) sat outside the
boots' answers, so a stall beside them still made the case INCONCLUSIVE.
Patching each rig would have been three copies of the rule, which is how it
had gone wrong in the first place. I proposed one rule in one place, and Hans
agreed: `boot` takes the rig's `inspect(copy, problems)` and
`check(lines, count)` and folds both into the boot's answer, and a `Tally`
counts failed cases and stalled boots and refuses a case settled and never
closed.

The third max review found no wrong verdict, and found that the self-test
could not see the order that brings the bug back, because the fake's
`aborts()` did not stop the guest as the real one does. Following that down
turned up an older defect in the classifier itself: `firmware_stalled` took
the transcript's last line, and after a stop that line is QEMU's own
`terminating on signal 15 from pid ...`. So `Guest.aborts()`, which prints
a note naming a stall for the rigs not on `boot_stalled`, had never once
been able to print it. The rigs on `boot_stalled` read the transcript before
the stop and were never affected, which is why nothing had shown it.
`firmware_stalled` now passes over the signal line wherever it lands.

The fourth max review found the one live regression of the branch:
`boot_stalled` had learned to refuse a label used twice in a run, and
`test-volume-bound`'s `NAME...` command line, given a name twice, crashed
after both boots on that refusal. It also found `per_run` silently dropping
a sector the graded boot read fewer times, since `Counter` subtraction
floors at zero. The first fix for that refused on any sector of the disk,
and it promptly failed `test-write-walk`'s FAT32 pair on exFAT sector 2329,
read 14 times in the base boot and 12 in the graded one, nowhere near the
FAT the rig grades. The refusal was scoped to the graded region
(`8b65b79a`), and the high review that evening found its whole-number test
still unscoped, so `per_run` now bounds the difference itself
(`beee8acb`). The same high review settled which errors from reading a
damaged disk count as findings: the families a parser raises on bad bytes
(`ArithmeticError`, `LookupError`, `ValueError`, `OSError`, `struct.error`,
`RecursionError`, `SystemExit`), with anything else a bug in the rig that
keeps its traceback. An earlier round had used a narrower list and missed
the `IndexError` that `stale_cursor.Volume`'s `read(1)[0]` can raise.

Two measurements came out of the day and went to the roadmap rather than the
code. QEMU 11.1.2 writes its `-d` log a line at a time: read every two
seconds mid-boot, the file always ended on a newline and its sizes were not
4 KiB multiples, and nothing was lost after a SIGKILL, so the comments
saying it buffered were wrong and were rewritten. And every real firmware
stall in `scratch/firmware-stall-runs.log` is a boot of a copy of
`espexfat.img`, 4 of 508 exFAT boots against 0 of 420 others when the fourth
review counted, and three of those four were the traced rigs' (3 of their 72
exFAT boots, against 1 of 436 untraced). A fifth came that evening,
`test-write-walk:fat32-same-more`, exFAT and traced again. Whether the image,
the trace or both is a loop of boots away, and is a roadmap item.

The last rerun of the rigs produced a false lead worth recording. Running on
battery, the Mac fell to 3% and slept, from 19:49 to 19:59 and from 20:02 to
20:17 (`pmset -g log`). A spawn-reads step timed out across the sleep, a
FAIL and the right verdict for a step that did not finish, and one
listing-walk boot was logged as a firmware stall a second after the wake: a
QEMU suspended in the firmware and woken past the 90 s step timeout looks
exactly like one. Both rigs passed once the machine was on AC. That entry
is excluded from the stall counts, and the roadmap now says to check
`pmset -g log` before counting one.

Five rounds of review in all (four max and one high), and from the third on
none found a wrong verdict; what they found were gaps in the self-test, traps
for the next rig, and diagnostics. Every mutant of the last two rounds, 24
and 6, fails the self-tests. #350 was merged on the morning of the 26th.
What it deferred is on the roadmap: moving `disktrace.py`'s generic half
(boot, classify, `settle`, `Tally`) into `drive-qemu.py` before the next
multi-boot rig takes on the accounting, a stub QEMU so `make test` exercises
the real `aborts()` and `stop()`, rerunning a case by name, and the 26
per-boot logs `build/` never sheds.
