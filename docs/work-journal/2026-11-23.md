# 2026-11-23

## Five reviews of #504, and each found the last one's repair

`make test-net-dial` went in yesterday with `dial`'s slot leak fixed. Today
was its reviews, all at high, and the shape of them is this file's day.

The first review found that netd freed a `/net/tcp` slot on the pass after
it went `Closed`. The next clone could take that number, and the first
client's late `close` then shut the new client's connection. The fix gave
`pump_dials` a `Reap` mode: the `/net/tcp` table frees a `Closed` slot only
once its client has written `close` (`Wire::released`), or at the idle reap.
Checking it needed a client that holds a slot across a failed connect and
then clones again, in one process, and no shell tool can: every read of
`clone` allocates a slot, so `cat /net/tcp/clone` took all three, and
ulib's bulk write refuses the local `/net`. That is why `dialprobe` exists.

The second review found the first fix's gaps: an accepted connection that
`listen` never handed out had no client to release it, `close` on a slot
never opened waited for nothing, and `connect` could be written twice. It
also found that `serve` had `dial`'s bug, which became #505. The third found
the second fix's rule (free a pending connection on `Closed`) losing a
request that arrived with its FIN before `listen` was read. The fourth found
the third fix's `Closed` arm using up a reconnecting client's SYN. The fifth
found the fourth fix passing on every bare SYN, so a late copy of the first
connection's SYN opened a connection with nobody behind it, and found the
idle reap closing a slot without releasing it, holding it a second 12 s.
Each fix had a control, a mutation that made its new check fail, and each
control did; the controls proved the checks could fail, not that the rule
was whole.

After the fifth I said the pattern out loud: four rounds in a row, every
defect in the same few lines, when a slot is freed and which segments reach
it, and those lines have no host tests, only a rig that reaches some states.
I proposed fixing the two and stopping, with the rules taken out into a pure,
host-tested function as the next step, and Hans agreed. The fifth round's
fixes went in with their control (the `Closed` arm passing every SYN failed
the new check and the reconnect check) and without a sixth review. The
idle-reap release has no control: seeing it takes two 12 s idle periods.

## `serve` gives its listener back (#505)

Stacked on #504. `serve` wrote `close` only on success too, and a
`Listening` slot is never idle-reaped, so three `serve`s that timed out
refused every `dial` and `serve` until reboot. Its review found that a
listener's `close` left the connections it had accepted and never handed out
naming the freed slot as their parent, so the next `serve` there was handed a
connection to the old port; netd now resets and frees them. Two first
versions of that rig check passed against the bug: the unfinished second
client was freed by its SYN-ACK retries before the check looked, and waiting
for the first client's FIN discarded the RST that came before it. Both were
found by running the control, which is what the control is for.

Both PRs merged in order, #504 without deleting its branch and #505
retargeted to `main` first, and both branches were deleted after.
