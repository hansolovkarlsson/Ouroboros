## The code's pointers into CLAUDE.md, and a check for them

The first item of yesterday's standup: about ten kernel comments and two in
the Makefile sent the reader to "CLAUDE.md's ..." sections that had moved to
the CHANGELOG long ago, or with #386 to `docs/boot-history.md`. A grep found
thirty-four, in the kernel, the userland, the Makefile, `.cargo/config.toml`,
`programs/linker.ld`, `syscall-abi` and `np9p_client.py`, and #390 repointed
each to the document and entry that hold the story now. The old "GOP
framebuffer console, take N" sections are "round N-1" in the CHANGELOG, and
the pointers use the CHANGELOG's numbering.

The high review of that first commit is the part worth keeping. It found
pointers whose named entry existed but did not hold what the comment
promised (why kernel output corrupts `cond`'s screen is in the console
postmortem's "The handoff", not in the CHANGELOG's console entry), and one
comment that was simply false: `tasks.rs` said the runtime-region allocator
never frees and that no task destruction exists, while
`free_runtime_region` has given regions back in LIFO order since `exit`
landed. A pointer can name a section that exists and still be wrong.

Hans asked for the check that would have caught the rot, and #391 added
`scripts/check-doc-pointers.py`, run by `make test`. It finds every pointer
of the shape `X.md`'s "Name", wrapped across comment lines or not, and fails
when the file holds no heading or bold lead-in with that name. It found
about thirty more dead pointers in the documents, and `source-map.md`'s 26
"see ... above" references, which named sections of the `CLAUDE.md` it had
been moved out of. Three of my own mistakes showed up while building it:
the script failed on the example pointer in its own docstring and on one in
my Makefile comment; bold markers paired across a whole file let one stray
`**` shift every pairing after it (they now pair within a paragraph); and a
rewrap tool I wrote to reflow touched comments merged `fbconsole.rs`'s
"# Safety model" heading into its paragraph.

Its high review found the matcher too kind. It passed a name held anywhere
in any heading, so a renamed "Phase 4" stayed green on the cluster's 4a and
4b alone; a name that several anchors hold must now equal one of them whole,
and the eight pointers leaning on that name one entry each. The same review
caught a false claim in my own commit message: it said `architecture.md`'s
syscall-5 row was fixed, and the edit had never applied, because it sat in a
batch of replacements that stopped at an earlier failed match. The fix
commit says so. 150 pointers now, each naming a section. The check reads the
name, not what the section says, and the first review had shown why that
limit matters.

## gicv3.rs after multi-core

Repointing `gicv3.rs` showed three places still arguing from a single core:
the module doc's "even though this kernel is single-core", `SGI_BASE` called
"this CPU's" frame and nothing "genuinely concurrent", and `GICD_BASE` on the
"same single-core reasoning". #392 restates each against
`roadmap-smp.md`'s inventory: `SGI_BASE` is the boot core's frame (class B),
right only there and read by `enable_interrupt`, which `main.rs` calls on
the boot core alone; `GICD_BASE` is written before any `CPU_ON` (class A).
Its first version replaced the file's em dashes with semicolons, which the
rule does not list; they went in the next PR. #393 took `gic.rs`'s one em
dash.

## The no-em-dash sweep

`/style-check` found the rule in `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md` broken across most of
the tree: about 260 lines in code comments, `CLAUDE.md` and `README.md`, and
far more in `docs/`. The skill says to ask about dated accounts rather than
sweep them, and Hans settled it: the journal, archive, release notes,
handoffs, CHANGELOG, postmortems, `boot-history.md` and
`roadmap-completed.md` keep their punctuation as written, new text in them
follows the rule, and the living documents and comments are fixed. That is
now `CLAUDE.md`'s "Prose style" section (#394). The rest went one area per
PR so each stayed reviewable: the reference documents (#395), `ROADMAP.md`
(#396, 257 dashes), the cluster and fid-verbs plans (#398, #399), the
testing guides (#400), the research notes (#401), the remaining references
(#402), and nine site pages (#403). Each dash was rewritten by hand, by what
it was doing, and each file's semicolon count was compared before and after,
because semicolons were what I kept reaching for. One comment rewrap put
"1." at the start of a doc line, which clippy reads as a list item
(`doc_lazy_continuation`), and `make test` failed on it.

The scan itself had a hole. It came from the skill, written for Metaxis,
where a line starting with `>` is a quotation, and skips such lines.
`ROADMAP.md`'s callouts are `>` lines and are the document's own prose, so
#396 reported zero dashes with sixteen still in them. They went in #397, and
the scan reads `>` lines from then on. Two quotations in the cluster phase
plans quoted sentences of `roadmap-cluster.md` that #398 had just reworded;
they follow their source now rather than keeping the old dash. Where a dash
belongs to someone else's words, it stays and the line says
`<!-- as written -->`: the Helix project's own description, and the title of
a Redox news post.

Five documents are abridged on the site, and editing them made
`check-site-freshness` red. Each was re-stamped only after a word-level diff
of old against new showed nothing but capitals after new periods and a few
connectives, so the pages still say what the sources say. The site's own
HTML then had 770 dashes across 19 pages; #403 swept the nine that abridge
living documents (348), giving every title a middle-dot separator and the
footer a comma. The other ten abridge postmortems, and Hans decided they
stay as written like the records they summarize; #404 names them in
`CLAUDE.md`'s "Prose style".

Every living Markdown file, 42 of them, now scans clean. The rule still has
no mechanical check here, as it does in Metaxis's `tests/hygiene.sh`, so the
guard is the user-level file and the next sweep.
