# 2026-11-04

## The first real stick, and a read-back that macOS wrote into

The standup's first item was the `erase disk` GPT fix, but Hans asked about
the one-disk stick for the Pi instead, so the day began there. `make
bootstick` had existed since 2026-10-14 and had only ever written image
files. `make test-bootstick` passed 48 of 48 on `main` first, and then Hans
ran it against the real Lexar stick, `/dev/disk12`, and it refused: "does
not read back what was written; do not boot it".

A `sudo dd` read-back compared with the image the run had kept differed in
six sectors of 182,272, all inside partition 1: FSInfo, the dirty bit in
both FATs, a `.fseventsd` entry in the root directory and its two clusters.
macOS had mounted the new ESP the moment `dd` gave the disk a table, and
written to it before the comparison. The MBR and the ext2 root were exact.
The rig could not have seen it: it attaches every image with `hdiutil
-nomount`, so nothing ever mounted. On an image attached the way a real
stick is, the same race lost the other way, the tail's zeroing refused as
busy. The fix in #406 wrote the disk with sector 0 blank and the MBR last,
after the read-back, and the rig gained a disk macOS may mount, with the
mount itself checked as the observer.

The review of that fix moved the tail's zeroing ahead of the image to close
a backup-GPT window. The second review saw what that reorder did: the old
table was still in sector 0 during the tail write, and a real USB disk is
read again when a raw write ends, so macOS could remount the old ESP and
refuse the image's write. No image-file rig could tell the orders apart.
Sector 0 is now blanked first by a write of its own, and Hans re-staged the
real stick with it: written, read back, ejected. I had also deleted the kept
image during the reproduction, the only copy of the old stick's `/etc`;
nothing was lost, because the comparison had shown the stick held it, but
the deletion was not mine to make without saying so, and I said so.

## The Pi on the stick, and a console that depended on the display

The re-staged stick booted the Pi 4 with no card: `USB boot mode 2`, the
firmware read off the stick, the kernel naming the stick's partition 1 its
boot partition, `fsd` on the ext2 root. That answered the Pi's half of
one-disk step 0, `BOOT_ORDER`, by booting rather than reading it. The
serial capture stopped at `exiting boot services`, and Hans guessed the
monitor and keyboard were the reason. I said removing them would show less,
since the kernel had found no serial console (`NoDtb`, `NoSpcr`) and its
output after the exit was on HDMI only. He was right and I was wrong: with
neither attached the kernel found `console @ 0xfe201000 (via ACPI SPCR)`
and the whole session was on serial. Which of the two decides SPCR is not
known; they were removed together.

With the card back in, the firmware booted the card, the expected order,
but the card still held a kernel of 2026-10-11, older than one-disk step 1,
and it mounted the stick's ESP as `/`, writable. The roadmap had named that
hazard ("a Pi boot from a card and a stick of different builds") and here
it was. Re-staged at the same build, the card's kernel named the card's
partition its boot partition and `fsd` mounted the stick's root by its type.
Step 8 was done.

## Parallels: step 0, and a fault on every boot

Parallels' firmware listed the stick in Boot Manager and booted it when
chosen, but a boot order of `usb` alone went to the setup screen, twice.
Then the kernel faulted right after `keyboard ready`, a synchronous external
abort at `0xa000000`, from the stick and from a `.hdd` built from `main`
alike. The project's `\FBCON` flag put the post-exit lines on the screen and
showed the fault; the VM's own serial port, already configured to
`parallels/com.output.txt`, held the whole log once I thought to look.

The cause was a heuristic the kernel had carried since 2026-08: scan QEMU's
hardcoded virtio-mmio slots whenever a serial console was found before the
exit, since only QEMU had one. Parallels Desktop had updated to 27.1 the day
before, and its VM now had a console. The Pi had had one since 2026-09-28,
and had been reading RAM at `0xa000000` ever since, harmlessly, which is why
nobody knew the heuristic was already false. Hans chose the principled fix:
probe only what the firmware's DSDT declares, `_HID "LNRO0005"`.

The first build of that still faulted, at `0x2180000`. A temporary dump of
the AML around each `LNRO0005` showed Parallels declaring a virtio GPU there,
gated by a `_STA` method that reads a hardware field, and four NICs and a
balloon naming `LNRO0005` as their `_CID`. This VM has no virtio GPU, so the
method answers absent and the registers fault. Honouring `_STA` fixed
Parallels, and the stick then booted its own kernel there to its ext2 root,
`fsd` saying the boot partition was on the same disk. The NICs are a lead:
the roadmap has said Parallels networking needs virtio-PCI, and these are
declared as virtio-mmio.

## Four reviews, and the design that each one broke

The scan was reviewed four times today, and each review found a way to read
an absent device as present. The first version searched one device's bytes
for `_CRS` and `_STA`; the first review found a child device's objects read
as the parent's, and my fix skipped anything that looked like a child. The
second review reproduced that fix's hole: `5B 82` inside a constant, skipped
as a child, hid the device's own `Name (_STA, Zero)`. I replaced the search
with a walk of the device's own objects. The third review reproduced two
more ways past that walk (`5B 82` before the `_HID` taken for the device; an
`If` skipped whole) and named the one no device-local scan can see: a
`_STA` added by a later `Scope (\_SB.VR00)` or an SSDT. At that point the
patches were the defect, and the design changed: both tables walked
together from their top level, names resolved to absolute paths, two
passes. Hans took that recommendation.

That rewrite had a bug of its own that every test caught at once: `Path`'s
derived equality compared the whole fixed-size array, stale segments
included, so a parent never equalled its device. The fourth review, after
the merge, found an unbounded recursion (a root-relative `Scope` keeps the
path short however deep it nests), that a constant `_STA` is not constant
(load-time code or `\_SB._INI` can store to it), and that fields define
names; #408 bounded the nesting, trusts nothing under uncertainty or when a
method names `_STA`, and reads field units. It also made config reads
unspellable: `read_reg` takes a const offset below `REG_CONFIG`, and config
space comes only through the `Config<LEN>` handle `find_device::<LEN>`
returns. Two of my own slips are worth recording: a grep that filtered out
the xHCI lines made the Parallels fault look as though it came right after
`console live`, and a mutation reverted with `git checkout` took
`virtio_blk.rs`'s uncommitted edits with it, the trap my own memory note
names. The walk is now as strict as a scan without an interpreter can be,
answering absent wherever it cannot be sure, and QEMU's whole DSDT still
gives its 32 transports.
