# 2026-10-15

## The allocator DevTools asked for, and a calloc that zeroed -8 bytes

The day was one item from the standup, DevTools's nano-malloc note: picolibc's
nano-malloc keeps one address-ordered free list, so every `malloc`, `free`
and `realloc` walks it, and cpp on a large input is quadratic. The note
named a candidate, `-Dnewlib-nano-malloc=false`, newlib's binned malloc,
and the triage had promised to weigh its cost against the 1 MiB heap.

A measurement came first, so that the change could be seen. `/bin/CMALLOC`
times malloc, free and realloc at 500 to 4,000 slots with as many free
blocks scattered between the live ones, and `make test-malloc` grades the
largest count's cost over the smallest's. Against the committed library it
printed 6.4 times, and the rig failed, which was the point. Then the binned
build: CMEM still held 63 of its 64 blocks, the ratio fell under one, and
Edit and cpp both died on their first screen with a permission fault one
page into the stack. A diagnostic line printing the trap frame's registers
showed `x0` the heap's first chunk, `x1` 0 and `x2` -8: `memset(p, 0, -8)`,
from calloc. The disassembly had the whole story in four instructions:
clang had compiled newlib's calloc to `malloc(n * size)` and then a memset
of a constant -8 bytes, having taken the read of the fresh chunk's size as
a read of uninitialized memory.

picolibc guards against exactly this with `-fno-builtin-malloc` on its
malloc sources, and the flag was not on the command line. Its meson probe
had failed, and so, it turned out, had every optional-flag probe it makes:
the cross file put `--ld-path` on the compiler line, clang warned it unused
on every compile, and meson probes with
`-Werror=unused-command-line-argument`. Thirty of thirty-one flags have
probed NO since the port began on 2026-08-28; the one YES is
`-Wno-unused-command-line-argument`, which picolibc then adds, and which is
why the build always looked clean.

## The right fix, seen first by the project downstream

The first instinct was the root cause: move the link flags to
`c_link_args`, and every probe came good. The probes then also turned on
`long double`, `_Complex` and nine other features in `picolibc.h` and
installed `complex.h`. Within the hour DevTools filed a finding against
this working copy, not against `main`: its `cc` reads our headers straight
from `../../Ouroboros`, refuses `long double` here (binary128 in software),
and failed on `<math.h>` and `<wchar.h>`. It was right, and the headers it
relies on were fixed only by accident. The fix narrowed to the two malloc
flags passed by hand, the probes left broken with an item on the roadmap,
and the note answered the same day. A memory note now says that DevTools
reads the working tree.

## Three reviews, and the allocator put back

A high review of #301 found the timing rig weaker than it claimed: the
first count carried the start-up cost (the "0.6 times" printed beside
"about 110 ns at every size" could not both be true), the random draws and
the zero scan were timed, and only 16 bytes of a block were dirtied, the
16 that `free` overwrites. Reworking it found something worse. The
nano-malloc control could not run at all: on `main`'s library CMALLOC never
printed a line. The earlier control had run before CMALLOC called calloc,
and the new one walked into a hang. Printing every operation stopped it at
a `realloc`; rebuilding nano with the two flags made it finish; and
diffing the objects showed what the missing `-fno-builtin-free` had done:
clang deleted, as dead, the size nano-malloc writes on a chunk just before
it frees it, in `realloc`'s split of a remainder and in
`__malloc_grow_chunk`. Every C program on `main` links that allocator, Edit
and cpp among them.

The max review then went at the switch itself, and on this platform it did
not hold. newlib's malloc leaves `errno` 0 out of memory, relying on an
`sbrk` that sets `ENOMEM`, which ours does not, and cpp reads a failed
`fopen` with `errno` 0 as "already included" and drops the header without a
word. It refuses requests with half the heap free, asking `sbrk` for the
whole request when our `sbrk` will not give memory back. It adds 11 KB a
program against the 128 KB spawn buffer. And the gain could not be shown
here: inside 1 MiB cpp runs out of memory at about 70 KB of a macro-heavy
input under either allocator, and at 35 KB both finish in the same time.
So the recommendation turned around, Hans agreed, and #301 ships
nano-malloc rebuilt with the two flags, with the headers `main`'s byte for
byte. The speed half of DevTools's note went back to its inbox as
accepted, to be chosen with the per-program heap, which is what makes the
gain reachable.

The second max review found the new checks blind in their own ways (below,
and in the postmortem). `check-picolibc`, written to guard the defect,
passed `main`'s hanging library, since the same objects define `malloc` in
both. It now pins `libc.a` whole, which rebuilds byte for byte, and both
header trees DevTools reads. CMALLOC reads every block back before it frees
it, its pattern never 0, and grades the timed runs' refusals again. Six
controls, each failing on its own check.

DevTools sent a third note in the afternoon, asking for Edit and cpp to be
compiled and assembled by its own `cc` and `as`. Accepted, and blocked as
soon as it was measured: built that way `/bin/CPP` is about 238 KB, over the
spawn buffer, which is now its own roadmap item.
