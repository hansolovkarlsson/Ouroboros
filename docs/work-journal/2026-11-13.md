## The NIC enum, and a bound I made the device's (#454)

Step 3 of the Pi's network plan is a refactor: `nic.rs` holds
`enum Nic { Virtio }` and `syscall.rs` goes through it. I made one change
beyond the move. The frame bound the syscalls check had been
`virtio_net::MAX_FRAME` by name, and I made it the installed device's own,
`Nic::max_frame`, on the reasoning that a per-device constant should not be
spelled as virtio's.

The review took that apart. `netd`'s receive buffers are a fixed 1600
bytes, and `NET_RECV` refuses a buffer longer than the bound. A GENET arm
with a bound below 1600 would have had every receive refused, and `netd`
folds that refusal into "no frame", so the board would have sent and never
received, with no line anywhere. The bound is a number of the ABI again,
now named, `syscall_abi::NET_MAX_FRAME`. The same review found that
virtio-net's `poll_frame` indexes its buffer by the caller's length, kept
in range only by that bound and stated nowhere, and that `netd` indexes its
own buffer by the length `NET_RECV` returns, which is the frame's and not
the copy's. That last is older than the step and is `netd`'s; it is on the
plan under step 7.

My record of the step also said nothing reaches the driver around the enum
"since `syscall.rs` no longer names `virtio_net`". That does not follow,
and one of the two files named never did. The reason is that `init_net`
moves the one `Device` into the enum.

## The driver, on the host (#455)

Step 4 is the `genet` crate's driver logic, and its check asks for every
constant cited to its line in the source. The plan's facts had been read on
the 12th from files not kept, so I fetched U-Boot's driver at `v2024.10`
and Linux's at `v6.6` into `scratch/`, and wrote from them. `mac.rs` is
U-Boot's probe and start, write for write, and a model in the tests is
typed from the sources as numbers so a constant changed in the crate fails
there.

Reading two drivers side by side is what made the step more than a
transcription. They disagree, and each disagreement is a place where
following U-Boot alone trusts something: Linux masks the producer index to
16 bits, the high half being a discard count; U-Boot's wait for a send
compares against a producer it never wrapped, and so never ends at the
65536th frame; Linux drops a frame with error flags and U-Boot hands up
whatever the descriptor says; Linux turns on a 64-byte status block that a
driver following U-Boot would not expect to find left on. The crate
departs from U-Boot at each, and says so.

One of my own sentences did not survive the same reading. I had written
that the device does not pad a short frame, so the caller must, and built
a 60-byte minimum on it. Linux's transmit path says the opposite in a
comment: with append-CRC, no software padding is needed. I found it
looking for the line to cite. The minimum lasted an hour.

The mutation pass hung on its twenty-fifth mutation. With the producer
index compared unmasked, a test's drain loop never ended: a mutation
caught, by a script with no timeout. The pass left one mutation alive,
U-Boot's own "consumer at or past the producer" in place of equality, and
it got the wrap test that fails it.

The review found no wrong offset. It found that `quiet` promised the
device would write no more memory and only cleared the enable bits, as
U-Boot does, where Linux polls a status register until the DMA says it
stopped. It found that a PHY status of all ones read as a gigabit link,
that a release too many would put the consumer past the device, and that
the crate could return a frame of one byte. After those fixes the mutation
pass left two alive, both on the same line: "no longer than its buffer",
which the new bound of 1536 had made unable to fail. The line was removed.

## What U-Boot does to the PHY (#456)

The same reading found something the plan had not known. U-Boot's bring-up
writes the PHY's RGMII clock delays through two Broadcom shadow registers,
resets the PHY, and reads the speed the PHY settled on from a Broadcom
status register. The plan had assumed nothing need be written to the PHY.
The two drivers also disagree on the MAC's own delay bit for this board's
mode. I put it to Hans as decision D7 with a recommendation, do what
U-Boot does, since taking its MAC setting without its PHY setting is a
combination nobody runs. He said yes.

Step 4b built it. The one real choice was the order. U-Boot sets the
delays and then resets; Linux resets and then sets them. I do not know
whether the reset keeps the delay settings. I kept U-Boot's order and
wrote that the PHY then "ends as it does under U-Boot either way".

The review, which fetched the sources itself and found the sequence right,
pointed out that the argument only holds if the PHY starts where U-Boot
finds it, and here it starts where the Pi's UEFI firmware left it. So
`setup` now reads both shadows back after the reset and returns them, and
the first board run answers the question I could not. It also found that
my reset poll took the controller's "no answer" flag as an error, which
U-Boot's read never looks at: a PHY silent while it resets would have
failed the set-up as "no PHY". And one of my tests asserted the shadows
after the model's reset, writing down as fact what the module doc said was
unknown. The model's reset can now keep them or wipe them, and the tests
run both.

## The kernel's half, and a MAC left off (#457)

Step 5 is the first code of the arc on the Pi's boot path.
`kernel/src/genetnic.rs` finds the one GENET the tables declare, makes it
quiet, sets it up through the crate, sets up the PHY and logs the link. I
left the MAC's transmitter and receiver off: the step is bring-up and
link, and sending and receiving are each their own trip to the board.

The plan says "quiet at the exit". I did it after the identity map is
installed. At the exit on the Pi the kernel is at EL2 on the firmware's
tables with no fault handler of its own, and a missed mapping there would
hang without a line.

QEMU has no GENET, so what it can hold is the other half: `test-netdump`
now requires one driver line, the receive area walking cacheable on all
128 pages, and no other. Two controls showed the rig can fail. With the
non-cacheable mapping forced, the area walked non-cacheable on 128 of 128
pages and `mmu.rs`'s own self-check agreed in all eleven views. With the
Pi's address taken where nothing is declared, the kernel faulted at
`0xfd580000`.

The review found the stuck case I had built: `has_frame` asked the device
while `poll_frame` returned nothing, so one frame reported would have
woken `netd` on every tick for good. It found that I skipped the link's
wait when the link bit was low before the PHY's reset, which a link still
coming up also reads as; the boot now waits up to 5 s every time, and a
board with no cable pays all of it. It found the device made quiet only
when it was also installed. And it found two things wrong beyond the
driver. The kernel's list of mapped devices held five, the Pi fills five,
and so the SD controller and the Ethernet were both mapped only because
the console's 1GB block holds them, the same coincidence fixed for the
console itself in September. And my record said the three copies of
"exactly one device" could not differ, since a list of one cannot have
dropped any. They can: `amlscan` counts candidates past its own limit as
dropped. The SD card's copy took one device whatever was dropped beside
it.

Both were fixed, and both reach past networking: the list holds seven, and
the SD card's discovery uses the shared rule. Each should leave a working
board as it was. Neither ran on one.

## The transmit clock delay

The question the board left was what `NOT as written` means for a frame.
The two sources answer it once the reading is put beside them. The
transmit clock needs one delay of about 2 ns, from the MAC or the PHY.
Linux has the MAC make it and writes the PHY's off, after the PHY's
reset. U-Boot has the MAC not make it (`ID_MODE_DIS` set) and also
writes the PHY's off, before the reset. Read as written, U-Boot has no
delay at all, and U-Boot works on this board. The board printed why: in
that order the PHY ends with its delay on. U-Boot's write is undone and
its driver works by the state the PHY comes back to.

This driver took U-Boot's order and U-Boot's `ID_MODE_DIS`, so it ends
where U-Boot does, with one delay. The line that read as a fault is the
state to expect, and the wrong repair is the obvious one: write the
delay off again after the reset, and there is none. Had the read-back
not been there, the module doc's "transmit clock delay off" would have
stood as what the PHY holds, and the first bad frame would have sent me
to turn off a delay that was the only one.

Two things the reading does not say: whether the reset restored the
delay or the write never took, and what the firmware left in the PHY.
`setup` reads both shadows before it writes them, so step 6 logs them.

## Step 6, the transmitter alone

Built the same afternoon, on the board's readings. Two choices in it are
worth their reasons.

The receiver stays off. U-Boot's start turns both enables on in one
write, and the crate's `start` did the same. But the moment the receiver
is on, the receive DMA writes to whatever addresses the descriptors
hold, and "the device takes a CPU address as its own" is still a
prediction from a missing `_DMA`. The transmit DMA only reads. So
`start` takes `Receiver::Off` or `Receiver::On` as an argument with no
default, the kernel passes `Off`, and a wrong address in this step is a
wrong frame that `tcpdump` shows, not a kernel written over. Step 7 is
then one variable too: the receiver.

The buffer's stores need a barrier. Mapping the transmit page
non-cacheable keeps the frame out of the cache, and I had written the
copy and the send before remembering that is half of it: a store to
Normal memory is not ordered against the Device write that tells the GENET
to read it. `xhci.rs` learned that on the Pi and has `dsb sy` inside
every register write. `send_frame` has one between the copy and the
crate's writes. QEMU would never have shown it missing, and here QEMU
cannot run the code at all.

`phy::setup` now returns the two shadows as it found them, which it had
always read and dropped, and the kernel prints them. The next boot says
what the firmware left in the PHY.

443 tests, 11 mutations of the new lines each failed by one, and
`test-netdump` passes with the transmit buffer's line. None of that
touches `send_frame`: no emulator has the device, so the kernel's half
of a send has run nowhere, and the board is its first run. The check is
`tcpdump` on the Mac, best over a direct cable, since a switch drops a
frame with a bad checksum and then "nothing arrived" does not tell a bad
frame from none.
