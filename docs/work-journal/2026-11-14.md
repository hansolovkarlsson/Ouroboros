## The barrier as a function, and the stop given a line (#465)

Two of the four findings #463's review left open were small enough for a
morning, with one item from the board's first runs.

The review had said nothing guards the `dsb sy` in `send_frame`: it was
an inline instruction, and a later edit could drop it with every check
green. The xHCI driver's answer to the same problem is a naked function
of fixed instructions that `make check-xhci-barriers` finds in the built
image. The plain copy of that would not do, because the check wants each
sequence exactly once and `dsb sy; str w1, [x0]; ret` is already the
xHCI's. I gave the GENET's store its base and offset as two operands,
`str w1, [x0, x2]`, which is a natural shape for a bus that holds a base
and is handed offsets, and is a different sequence. Every register write
the driver's bus makes now goes through it, so the barrier is before the
set-up's writes too and not a send's alone. The inline instruction is
gone. Three mutations failed the check as they should.

The stop's line was the other half: on 2026-11-13 "it stopped" had been
read from the absence of a line. `quiet` now says how long the stop took.

I told Hans before the review that this change sits on the frame path
and that nothing here can run it, and that I would hold its merge until
the adapter run had been read on #463's code. One variable a round trip.

## Two reviews of it, and what each found

The first review found no fault in the path and eight things. The one
that mattered was that the check's "one caller" was a comment. The check
asks for the store once and at least one call to it; with a second
caller, `Mmio::write` could be routed around the barrier and the check
would stay green, and step 7 is exactly where a second caller would
appear. The bus and its store went into a private `mod bus`, as
`xhci.rs` has `mod reg`, and a fourth mutation shows a caller outside it
does not compile. It also found that my clock read divided by the
counter's frequency before the stop, so a firmware that left the
frequency at 0 would have ended the boot ahead of the one thing `quiet`
is for; and that "every GENET register write" was false, the scan in
`netscan.rs` having a store of its own.

The second review read the fixes alone and found no fault in the code
and eight statements that did not match it. Four were mine from that
hour. The worst: I had written in the plan that printing the status the
stop read needed a change to the `genet` crate. Both status registers
are public and `quiet` holds the bus. Had that stood, the next board
trip would have gone ahead without a reading two lines of code could
give. They are printed now. The stop's line had also promised that a
transmit DMA not yet stopped "is asked again after the MAC's reset",
which is `Genet::init`'s to do and which several paths never reach; the
line now says what was seen and promises nothing.

After the second round I proposed stopping. Each round had found what
the round before wrote, and a third automated review would mostly have
read the second round's sentences. The diff went to Hans to read
instead.

Two things are left open outside this driver, both found by the second
review's reading and neither reachable on the Pi as it runs:
`publish_cycle_word` in `xhci.rs` sits outside `mod reg`, its one caller
held by the convention `mod bus` was made to end here; and
`timer::monotonic_us` divides by the counter's frequency unchecked.

## The adapter run: the frame on the wire

The adapter arrived in the afternoon. The run was of `660aebe3de30`,
#463's code with the morning's records and nothing of #465.

The run of 2026-11-13 had failed as an observation, not as a test: the
capture was on the Wi-Fi, where a bad frame, no frame and a good frame
all look like nothing. So this one was set up to show the capture
something first. `tcpdump` ran on the adapter's interface with no
filter, started before the Pi had power, and the instruction was to
watch it before the serial console: once the link came up, the Mac's own
DHCP and ARP on that interface would print, and if they did not, a
silent run would mean nothing. They printed, 211 frames of them.

The 212th was the Pi's. One broadcast ARP request from
`d8:3a:dd:85:24:22`, 60 bytes: the 42 that `netd` builds, byte for byte
in the capture's hex, and 18 zeros of the device's padding. The serial
log had `first frame sent: 42 bytes, taken by the device` at the same
`ping`, and the adapter counted no input error. Three predictions became
readings at once: U-Boot's pairing of the clock delays is right on this
board, the device pads a short frame, and the DMA takes the CPU's
address of the buffer as its own. The last is the one step 7 was held
for, since the receive DMA will write to the address it is given.

One prediction was wrong, and it was in the plan and in the standup in
my words: that the frame would ask for the gateway, `10.0.2.2`. It asked
for `192.168.0.1`, the address pinged. `netd`'s `ping` hands its target
straight to `arp_resolve`, and only the TCP and UDP paths go through
`next_hop`, whose own comment says so. I had described what a routed
stack does and not read what this one does. Nothing depended on it this
time: the pass was the MAC address and a whole frame. It would have
mattered had the frame been filtered for by its target.

What one frame does not show is in the plan: a second send, a longer
frame, anything received.

The run went in as #466. With it read, the reason for holding #465 was
gone, and both merged, #466 first so the history has the wire check
before the change to the path it checked.
