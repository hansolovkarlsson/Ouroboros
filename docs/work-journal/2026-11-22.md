## A disk mounted after boot gives netd its files (#500)

The item came from the Pi and Parallels: a stick that arrives after the
boot is mounted by `mount -a`, but `netd` had already looked for `/etc/net`
and `/etc/cluster` with nothing mounted and kept the default address and no
identity until the next boot. The first question was whether a load at boot
could be early by accident. It cannot: `fsd` mounts tree 0 before it takes
its first request, and the kernel queues a call to a task not yet
receiving, so a boot-time load that answers `NO_FS` has seen what the boot
holds. The late disk always comes by `mount -a`, which is the one place to
hook.

So `fsd` sends `netd` a one-way notice, `NETOP_ROOT_MOUNTED`, each time tree
0 mounts, by `MSG_SEND`, never a call. `netd` takes it from `fsd`'s
slot alone and reloads only when a read of its last load went unanswered.
`test-net-config` gained a boot whose root is on a stick only `mount -a`
brings in, checked by the address netd answers HTTP at before and after.
The high review found eight things; five were fixed, two declined with the
reasons written into the roadmap item, one folded into a fix.

## `test-long-set`'s red was the rig's reader (#502)

Yesterday's closeout left `make test-long-set` failing on main before it
booted. The cause was not the image and not fsd. `exfatimg.Volume` held one
buffered handle while the rig wrote the image through others, and at
Python's default buffer of 128 KiB the buffer held the whole root, so
`plant()` read the root as it was before `fill_deleted` and wrote that back.
Shown on a copy: after the fill, the open handle read `0x05` where a fresh
one read `0x85`. `Volume` opens unbuffered now. When the default buffer grew,
and so when the rig turned red, is not known.

## A synchronous op's wait keeps what it does not take (#503)

The second review of #493 had left this: while netd waited inside an op of
its own (an ARP, a ping, a DNS query, a fetch), every TCP frame that was not
the op's was dropped, so a SYN arriving then went unanswered. Hans chose the
design, keep and replay. `recv` takes a `Backlog` and
a test for the op's own frames, both required, so no polling loop can drop a
frame by forgetting; the kept frames go to `on_frame` after each drain. The
review made every wait credit the backlog and kept only TCP. A first-run
puzzle, SYN-ACKs coming back 0.0 s after their SYNs in the fetch cases, was
traced rather than explained away: the whole fetch takes about 3 ms, and both
SYN-ACKs leave after its last frame, which is the replay's order.

## The first `/net/tcp` connect on a wire (#504 opened)

The last item of the day was the roadmap's open fix "no rig sends a
`/net/tcp` `connect`", left by #490. `make test-net-dial` puts the script at
10.0.2.2 on a socket netdev and builds every frame itself. Its first run
found a defect in `dial`: it wrote `close` only on success, so each failed
dial held its slot for netd's 12 s idle reap, and three of them refused the
next dial anyone ran. The branch was pushed with the fix and its control;
its reviews were the next day's work.
